## The Agentic Enterprise Thesis

Enterprises are evolving from hierarchical command structures to networks of autonomous, AI-augmented capability domains. This isn't a technology shift — it's an organisational architecture shift.

Key principles (from the published essay series):
1. **Skills, Not Departments** — Organise around capabilities, not org chart boxes
2. **Intent Over Assignment** — Define outcomes, let autonomous agents figure out how
3. **Institutional Memory** — Knowledge lives in systems, not people's heads
4. **Events, Not Reports** — React to what happened, don't wait for someone to summarise it
5. **Operate and Modify** — Every system must be runnable AND changeable by its team
6. **Standards That Don't Strangle** — Governance enables, it doesn't block
7. **Governance as a Service** — Policy as code, not policy as committee
8. **Scripts, Not Documents** — Executable specifications replace static docs
9. **Feedback in Days** — Shorten every loop to days, not quarters
10. **The One-Person Company** — Every individual can leverage AI to operate at team scale

## The Requirements Problem

AI coding tools make teams build 3x faster. But 40% of what they build is wrong because requirements are shaped in PowerPoint, filtered through 4 handoffs, and arrive at developers as ambiguous tickets.

**Epic as Code** is a discipline (not a tool) that structures requirements as executable, version-controlled specifications. The format matters less than the discipline: requirements that are testable, traceable, and owned by the team that implements them.

## The Federation Problem

Enterprises can't fit in one monorepo — but splitting repos destroys AI context. Large language models work best with coherent, bounded codebases. When context is scattered across 47 repos with unclear boundaries, AI tools produce worse results.

The **Federation Model** partitions codebases by capability (not by team or technology) while preserving cross-boundary context through explicit contracts and shared schema.
